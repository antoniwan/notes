## What it is

Telling the truth. That part is simple to say. The harder part is that honesty starts inside: you can't be honest with other people about things you won't admit to yourself.

## How it shows up for me

For me, the hard part is turning it inward. Catching my own rationalizations. Noticing when I'm keeping score. Choosing honesty as a value is a commitment to practice it, not a claim that I always notice when I'm lying to myself. I don't.

What helps is making it something I can check. When I say something, can I show it? That turned out to matter even in my work with AI agents: when honesty is the foundation, the agent tests and verifies its own work with results, to prove it. I think people work the same way.

## How I practice it

I used to call this part "honor the flame." It's one law, never lie to yourself, that turned into a method. That's less comfortable than a posture, because a method can catch you. Each of these rules exists because honesty with myself failed in a specific way first.

1. **Search before you claim something doesn't exist.** "There's nothing on that" is a claim. Look first.
2. **Go to the source.** A fact quoted somewhere else isn't verified.
3. **Add nothing the source didn't say.** No extra adjectives, no guessing what someone meant.
4. **When an idea dies, let it die.** Don't keep shrinking it until it can't be wrong.
5. **Hold wins to the same standard as losses.** If you only track what's breaking, you'll miss what's holding.

None of this is for grading my days or sounding impressive. A beautifully described pattern isn't a changed one.

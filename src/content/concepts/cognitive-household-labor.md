## What it is

Also called the mental load or invisible labor. It's the thinking part of running a home, separate from doing the chores: noticing the milk is low, remembering the dentist appointment, planning the week, deciding who does what.

Researchers have measured it task by task, asking families who does the _planning_ and who does the _execution_. In studies of American mothers, most felt solely responsible for organizing the family's schedules, even when they worked outside the home, and carrying sole responsibility for the kids' well-being was linked to feeling empty and less satisfied with life and partnership.

## How it shows up for me

Naming it changed how I look at "helping out." Doing a chore you were asked to do is execution. Noticing it needed doing, before anyone asked, is the other half of the work. I try to own whole tasks, from noticing to done.

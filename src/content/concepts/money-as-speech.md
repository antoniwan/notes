## What it is

"Money as speech" is shorthand for the reasoning in two U.S. Supreme Court cases, _Buckley v. Valeo_ (1976) and _Citizens United v. FEC_ (2010): spending money to spread a political message is a form of speech, so caps on that spending restrict speech.

The rebuttal I find strongest: if amplifying a message is speech, then there's no natural stopping point. The loudest wallet wins, and that's not what free speech was supposed to protect.

## How it shows up for me

I believe in free speech deeply, and that's exactly why this bothers me. My voice and a billionaire's budget aren't the same kind of thing, and pretending they are makes my voice smaller.

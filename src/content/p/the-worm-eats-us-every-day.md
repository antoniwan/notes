I have to start with the game, because the game is brilliant. [_Dune: Awakening_](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1172710/) is one of the best games I've ever played, the exploration, the travel, the transitions, the vehicles, the systems, oh my Universe, and I crafted my first stillsuit in it, which was very cool.

Then Shai-Hulud ate me. I had so much stuff on me, all the advanced components, the unique weapons, everything, and it went down into the sand with the worm. I remade my kit with normal stuff, went back out, and the worm ate me again, and I was just standing there going, it cannot be, I've run this thing, I did it twice before, I had no trouble crossing this desert. Baffled. Done. I'm quitting, because the patience and the time this game asks for are in scarcity in my life right now, and I'm not going to spend them there.

Confession before I go any further: I've never read the book. Everything I know about Dune comes from the movies, the games, my friend Nizar, who has read the novels, and whatever I've dug up on my own. So if you've never read it either, welcome. A stillsuit is a full-body suit that catches your sweat, your breath and your piss, filters it, and hands it back to you as drinking water through a tube near your mouth. That's the thing about Arrakis that gets me: the people living there solve the problem in front of them. The water, the blood, the materials, the scavenging, anything that gets spit out gets picked up and used again, because the desert is real and it will kill you, so the tech is real too. And the worm is real. It's the thing that eats you, and it's also the thing that makes the spice, and the spice is what the whole empire runs on.

There's a scene in the book, one I found digging into the lore, about who drinks what. Early on, the Atreides arrive on Arrakis and hold a formal dinner in their new house. The previous rulers, the Harkonnens, left a custom behind: guests wipe their hands on towels, drop them on the floor, and the beggars waiting outside get whatever water can be wrung out of the cloth. On a planet where water is life, the poor drink what the rich wiped off their fingers. Duke Leto ends it and orders cups of water for the beggars instead. Hold on to that duke, I'm coming back to him.

My reaction to all of it, written down half laughing:

> "the Dune lore has analogy to our current situation sooooo bad, and we don't live in a deadly desert that has giant worms... and have limited resources.... BUT WE LIVE LIKE IT?!??!"

Because that's the inversion, no? Arrakis has the desert and it has the stillsuit. We don't have the desert, and we live like we do anyway, rationing and scavenging and each one sipping from our own tube, and the tech that could make it easier never reaches us, because the billionaires and the trillionaires are hoarding everything. The way I picture it, we're all in stillsuits, all of us, and the rich people are up there, looking down from the sky, going "hey, look at the peasants drinking their own piss," while they eat salmon or whatever it is rich people of this level eat, I wouldn't know.

I know the "we" needs care. Some people on this planet are living real deprivation right now and some of us can buy a lot of insulation from it, so sharing a planet doesn't put us all in the same desert. And some limits are real, water and climate among them. But a lot of what we live as scarcity isn't the desert. It's the arrangement, and I wrote that half down too:

> "we are not prioritizing their conservation, resourcing, nurturing, reparation, and careful distribution... we let capitalistic interests (Spice) take over"

And I have my own stillsuit. I make a good salary, and I'm still a blue-collar employee, and I still don't own my own home, have very few savings, am still paying off debt from a previous marriage, and honestly, my first instinct is to say that's on me, I take all the responsibility, and some of it is, but I also know I got here in the dark, nobody handed me the information or the knowledge or the support, I figured most of it out the hard way, the way my dad did, because he was an orphan and he was deprived of things too, and everybody is deprived of something, and that's the point, no? Why are we living like this? Who are the Fremen among us?

My first reaction to the Fremen was a joke:

> "fremen are the ultimate stoics, they would thrive in capitalism, would end up ruling the world jaj!"

It's a joke, and it's also true. The Fremen are the perfect stillsuit people, and a system that runs on scarcity loves nothing more than people who are proud of how little they need. We admire endurance, we reward it, we put it on posters, and admiring people for how well they endure is a pretty good way of asking them to keep enduring.

Then the story pulls the rug, and my other note from that morning was this:

> "when you realize the empire is bad and the fremen are brainwashed by a religion that was planted thousands of years ahead by an elite cabal of powerful women..."

That's the story. The Bene Gesserit seeded the Fremen's prophecy on Arrakis generations earlier, so when Paul and Jessica show up the religion is already waiting for them. Somebody engineered the future of an entire people, thousands of years out, and the people living inside it got a messiah and a holy war. And it isn't only them. The Emperor is planning, the Harkonnens are planning, the Guild is planning, and even the Fremen, the ones I just praised for solving today, are hiding water for generations so a planetologist's dream of a green Arrakis can come true someday. Everybody in that story is engineering tomorrow. I asked myself whether they all think they're preventing something worse, and of course they do, they almost always do, and it settles nothing.

That's the problem, and it's the same one we keep seeing over and over in all these stories: we forget about the humanity, and we get individuals with ideas about how everything should be run. Your reasoning, your human condition, is not to fucking over-engineer everybody else's future, bro. Think about now. Solve the problems you have now. Focus on the humans in front of you. Frank Herbert, who wrote the thing, said something close to it outside the book: [he warned against handing our judgment to leaders we admire, heroes included](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/ch03.html).

Which brings me back to the duke. Leto was a good man, and his cup was the right call, and it was still one man deciding, at his own table, who gets to drink. The next one in that chair could take it back, and in the story the next one in that chair is a Harkonnen. I'd rather not wait around for a better duke. I want the cup to be ordinary, just there, today, for whoever is thirsty at the door, without anybody's grand plan attached to it. That's what love, real love, the one that acts, looks like to me. It pours the water now, without waiting for the future.

And I keep thinking about that worm. It ate me twice in a game I thought I had figured out, and I got to walk away. The worm is eating us every day. When are we going to be done? When does somebody stand up in the sand and say, hey, I'm done being eaten by the worm?

The stillsuit was still very cool, though.

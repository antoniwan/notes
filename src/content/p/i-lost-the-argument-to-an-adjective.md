I want to start with what I actually said, because editing it out would be a lie:

> "this is not AI, and GAI??? not even close, this is madness to be marketed as AI"

That was me. Angry, a little reprehensive, arms crossed. I was fighting the category. LLMs are pure probability, no? Token in, distribution out, sample, repeat. Where's the intelligence in a slot machine with a vocabulary? I called it a category error and I felt smart saying it. I even had a joke ready: this stuff needs a label like food does. Non-GMO intelligence. Grass-fed cognition. The real thing, like mine.

Hold that thought, because the joke is going to turn around and bite me.

## The fight

My argument was clean and it felt righteous. Intelligence is understanding, and understanding can't be prediction, and these things are prediction machines, therefore not intelligent, therefore the marketing is madness. QED, cierra la libreta.

Except I was using these tools every day. For work. For tasks. For thinking, honestly, like right now. And they were helping. Not helping like a hammer helps, where the intelligence is all mine and the tool is dumb weight. Helping like a colleague helps. Catching things I missed. Pushing back. The Tuesday afternoon workload did not care about my philosophy. The theory said "not intelligence" and the workload said otherwise, and the workload won.

There's a name for what I was doing, apparently. [The AI effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_effect): every time a machine does something, we redefine intelligence as whatever machines still can't do. Chess wasn't real intelligence once a computer won. Translation wasn't. I was just the latest guy moving the goalposts and calling it rigor.

## The label

I lost the argument to the word's own adjective, which is embarrassing.

Artificial does not mean fake. It means made by us instead of grown. Nobody claimed these systems were natural, whole, human intelligence. The term conceded my objection before I raised it. Artificial light is real light. You can read by it, you can grow tomatoes under it, and nobody confuses it with the sun. Artificial flowers only look like the thing and do none of its work. Artificial sweetener is the strange middle case: real sweetness, zero nourishment.

So the question was never "is this intelligence, yes or no." The question is which kind of artificial we got. Light, flower, or sweetener. I still don't know. Some days it reads like light. Some days like sweetener, all taste and no calories. But "is it in the category" turned out to be the wrong question, and the right one is quieter: what does this do next to MY intelligence? It complements it. It is not it. It doesn't have to be it to have value. A ladder doesn't have to be legs.

That shift, from membership to relation, is the whole essay, really. Everything else is me catching myself.

## The habit

I noticed something while writing this, and it stings.

"Pure probability" was a claim that ran ahead of the data. The mechanism is probabilistic at the output, sure, but the internals build representations, [plan ahead of the token](https://www.anthropic.com/research/tracing-thoughts-language-model), do things "pure" was supposed to rule out. I said "pure" because it made my anger sound like analysis.

And then, in the very same breath where I surrendered that one, I did it again. I wrote that we are "starving our natural resources" to feed these tools. Dramatic. Felt true. The data: [data centers used about 1.5% of global electricity in 2024](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai/executive-summary), heading toward maybe 3% by 2030. That is not planetary starvation. The real story is uglier in a smaller way: the costs are local and concentrated, specific counties with strained grids and drained aquifers, water and heat and carbon left off the invoice, growth compounding at speeds no regulator is matching. That's bad enough. I didn't need to inflate it.

So there's the habit, named: when I'm angry, I reach for the biggest available claim. "Pure probability." "Starving the planet." "Madness." The overclaim feels like conviction from the inside. From the outside it's just a position asking not to be checked. I have to be able to catch this in real time or every essay I write is going to be a fight followed by a retraction, jejeje. Better to build the retraction in.

## Three surrenders

So this is where the piece actually lands, and it lands on surrender, which is not a word I use comfortably.

Surrender number one: the category. It really is artificial intelligence. The adjective was honest the whole time; I just never read it. AI can be many things. That's what I'm learning. The category was bigger than the fight I was having about it.

Surrender number two: knowing it all. I argued from the mechanism as if the mechanism settled the question, and I didn't understand the mechanism as well as my confidence implied. Humility, not as a virtue I perform, but as the accurate description of my position.

Surrender number three: innocence about cost. The tools are real and the bill is real. Who can afford this assistance, and what does the grid pay so I can have a thinking partner at 11pm? Those are questions of distribution and cost, and they don't care whether I win the word fight.

## Have we done this before?

Yes. In 1865 a man named Jevons wrote _The Coal Question_ and found the paradox that carries his name: make the engine more efficient and you burn more coal, not less, because efficiency makes the machine cheap enough to use everywhere. Every chip generation does more per watt, and total consumption climbs anyway. Cheaper cognition finds new uses the way cheaper steam did. My third surrender has a 160-year-old twin.

And now they want to put the datacenters in orbit. SpaceX [filed in January](https://www.engadget.com/science/space/spacex-wants-to-launch-a-constellation-of-a-million-satellites-to-power-ai-needs-175607771.html) to launch up to a million satellites of compute, sun-powered, costs relocated to somewhere the invoice is even harder to read. We lit the 19th century on whale oil until kerosene showed up, so maybe substitution saves us again. Or maybe it's the same bet with better marketing, madness with an FCC filing number. See, there's the habit again. Let me say only what I know: the costs are real, growing, and unpriced, and efficiency will not save us, because it never has.

I started this angry that they called it intelligence. I'm ending it worried about coal. That's probably the most honest trade I've made all year.
